US leads Australia leads

From 2000 through 2011, more Phase 1 clinical trials per million residents were typically run in the US than Australia.

But Australia has led every year since 2012, and the gap has only widened ever since.

When we swap the y-scale to look at absolute numbers of Phase 1 trials, the US is still ahead by far, but growth has leveled off since 2016, whereas Australian trials have more than doubled in that time frame.

But that’s not the end of the story. If we zoom in on Australia’s clinical trials . . .

. . . and break them down by the country where the sponsor is headquartered, a different picture emerges. For every year since 2014, the largest share has belonged to the US; in recent years, that share has approached or reached 50% of all Australian trials. Australia, other countries, and China follow.

Note: Phase 1 trials are counted by start year, 2000–25. Australian figures combine information from ClinicalTrials.gov with information from the Australian New Zealand Clinical Trials Registry (ANZCTR); US figures are from ClinicalTrials.gov only. Trials with intervention type “Behavioral” in ClinicalTrials.gov and “Educational / counselling / training” in ANZCTR have been excluded. Sponsor country is the country where the trial's lead sponsor is headquartered, not the trial site—every trial shown in the stacked area chart ran in Australia. When the lead sponsor is a subsidiary, the sponsor country is the global headquarters of the parent company. Sponsors headquartered in Hong Kong or Taiwan are counted toward China.
Source: ClinicalTrials.gov, ANZCTR, and UN World Population Prospects 2024
